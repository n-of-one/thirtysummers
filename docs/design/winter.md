# Between summers

What the map does while the family is away, and what the family does in
winter. Like the summer document, this is a first guess to be played.

## What the map does without you

**Resources come back at different rates.** Everything in the inner ring,
inside the first stream, is back every summer: fruit, feathers, sticks
where saplings stand, vines in the mud. That is the safe option, and it
means there is always enough within reach to pay the upkeep. Things that
only sell for gold, ore and shells beyond the first stream, come back
slowly, a share each winter, so a field worked hard one year gives less
the next and the family is driven outward to find the next one.

**Barriers grow back.** A cut thicket tile that touches uncut thicket has
a chance each winter to grow back, so a path through a wall narrows at its
edges and a path with open ground on both sides stays. A felled copse is
saplings again after a few winters, so logs are a field that renews and
the axe keeps its use.

**Built things wear, slowly.** A bridge loses one tile every other winter,
so repair is half the work of building and not a yearly chore. Wells never
decay. Caches stay where they are.

**Trails form.** Underbrush the family crosses often turns to grass over
the winters, gradually and with some chance, not every tile walked and not
all at once. The route the player prefers becomes the road, without a
tool.

## Winter

Winter is a screen, not a place. There is no clock. It shows what came
home, what winter costs, what the town sells, and a button for the next
summer. Nothing about winter is announced during the summer except the
two numbers the HUD carries all along: what winter needs, and what the
store holds.

In order:

1. **Keep or sell.** The store holds everything banked and everything
   carried when the clock stopped. The player chooses what to keep for
   tools and improvements: sticks and vines for a bridge, logs for the
   cart or a well. The rest sells at the ladder's prices, and the sum is
   shown with its breakdown.
2. **Upkeep.** Winter needs a number of fruit and a number of gold. Fruit
   comes from the store; if there is not enough, the shortfall is bought
   from the town at its price; if there is too much, the surplus sells
   like anything else. Gold comes off what was earned. Ending the summer
   away from camp adds to the gold owed.
3. **The shop.** What the town sells, with prices: tools, gear, and the
   things that need something carried home before the town will make
   them. Greyed when unaffordable. The price of what cannot be bought yet
   is what next summer aims at.
4. **The family.** Gold does not carry across winters. Whatever is left
   goes to the family, and the family has levels: each level needs more
   surplus than the last, accumulated over the summers. A level does
   nothing yet. Later it may unlock tools; it never raises upkeep. It
   gives the surplus a meaning, and it is what the map-arc calls family
   strength.

Upkeep rises only when a structure is finished, because the works have to
be supplied. Bridges, wells and caches are not structures, so in the first
five summers the upkeep is flat.

**Missing upkeep** is not the end of anything. The next summer starts with
max stamina ten lower. Felt on every rough tile, gone by the following
winter, enough to make upkeep a priority the year after.

## The start of a summer

A small notice at the start of each summer, and nothing more: how long
this summer is, and what winter will need. What grew back and what wore
is met on the map.

## Open

- What the shop holds in summers 1 to 5, and at what prices. The axe and
  the cart are in it; boots, clothes and the bigger backpack are
  candidates.
- The upkeep for summers 1 to 5, in fruit and in gold, and the town's
  price for fruit.
- How much a summer ended away from camp adds.
- The replenishment share for ore and shells, and the chance for thicket
  to creep and a trail to form.
- How many summers a felled copse takes to return.
