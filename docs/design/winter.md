# Between summers

This document describes what the map does while the family is away, and
what the family does in winter. The shop's stock, the upkeep and the tuning
numbers for the first five summers are in
[../current/five-summers.md](../current/five-summers.md).

## What the map does without you

**What comes back depends on where it is.** Everything in the near ring,
which is what camp can reach without crossing water, is back every summer:
fruit, feathers, sticks where a wood drops them, vines in the mud. That is the
safe option, and it is set to pay the upkeep and little more. The ring is
measured from wherever camp stands, so moving camp moves the ring. Beyond
the first stream, the ordinary things, fruit and feathers, come back by half
of what was picked. The valuable finds, shells and ore, do not come back at
all: a field that has been worked out is gone, and the family has to go
further out to find the next one. This never blocks play, because the near
ring always pays the upkeep, and there is always another field further out.

**Barriers grow back.** Each winter, a cut thicket tile next to uncut
thicket has a chance to grow back. So a path through a wall narrows at its
edges, while a path with open ground on both sides stays open. A felled
copse is saplings again after a few winters, so logs are a field that
renews, and the axe stays useful.

**Built things wear, slowly.** A bridge loses one tile every other winter,
so repairing it is half the work of building it and not a yearly chore.
Wells never decay. Over a life, better work lasts longer, as
[map-arc.md](map-arc.md) describes.

**What was left out does not keep.** Anything dropped on the ground is gone
by spring. Camp keeps everything. What is kept where is in
[summer.md](summer.md), under *Getting it home*.

**Trails stay.** Walking over underbrush during the summer treads it down
and wears it away, on the tiles the feet actually crossed, as
[summer.md](summer.md) describes. Winter does not undo this: a worn tile is
ground now, and the cart's road over the years is made of the same wear. A
winter does nothing at all to a trail, and that is what makes walking the
same line worth doing.

## Winter

Winter is a screen, not a place. There is no clock. It shows what came home,
what winter costs, what the town sells, where camp could stand, and a button
for the next summer. During the summer, nothing about winter is announced
except the list the player made on this screen.

In order:

1. **What sold.** Camp has everything stored there, plus everything that was
   carried when the clock stopped. Feathers, shells and ore sell, and the sum
   is shown with its breakdown. Building material does not sell at any price:
   sticks, vines and logs stay at camp for the builds they are for. So there
   is nothing to choose here, and no way to earn by carrying home the things a
   bridge is made of.
2. **Upkeep.** Winter needs some fruit and some gold, and how much of each
   depends on how the family lives. A family with nothing eats what it picks:
   a lot of fruit and little rent. A family with standing buys its food: less
   fruit each winter and more gold. The total never grows, and measured in
   backpack slots it shrinks, so reaching a level never makes a winter
   heavier. What changes is where the upkeep comes from: less from the ground
   near camp, and more from money that can only be earned further out, which
   is the direction everything else in the game points. Fruit comes from
   camp, a shortfall is bought from the town at its price, and a surplus
   sells like anything else. Gold comes off what was earned. Ending the
   summer away from camp adds to the gold owed.

   The ground near camp always has enough food, so upkeep is never a trap.
   At the levels where the rent is large, it is more than the ring's feathers
   make, and that is the point of it. A winter that went unpaid charges the
   level below the next year. So a bad year lowers the bar instead of raising
   it, and a run cannot spiral down on upkeep alone.
3. **The shop.** What the town sells, with prices: tools, gear, and things
   the town can only make once something has been brought home for them.
   Items the player cannot afford are greyed out. The town deals with a
   family of standing, so it stocks what the family's level allowed when the
   winter began. Items unlocked by the level reached this winter are shown
   frosted beside them, with their price, to be bought next winter. They are
   what next summer aims at. The first winter has no shop at all, not even
   frosted items: a new player looks at upkeep and the family and nothing
   else, and level 1 is what opens the town.
4. **The family.** Gold does not carry over from one winter to the next.
   Whatever is left goes to the family, and the family has levels. Each level
   needs more saved-up gold than the last, added up over the summers. A level
   opens more of the shop, changes how the winter is paid, and at one of the
   levels the family learns it can move. A level never makes a winter cost
   more than the one before. It gives the leftover gold a meaning, and it is
   what [map-arc.md](map-arc.md) calls family strength.
5. **Where camp stands.** Once the family is strong enough to pack up and
   move, winter is when they do it. The screen shows the ground the family
   has walked, and the player pitches camp on one of the places that can take
   a camp: open ground, within reach of water, with food growing around it.
   It costs material, which is one of the things material is for. Everything
   stored at camp comes along.

   Moving camp is a trade, not a shortcut. Everything a camp can reach
   without crossing water comes back every summer. So a camp on the far bank
   makes the far bank the ground that renews, and leaves the old ring to come
   back by half. It shortens every walk in the coming summers, and it gives
   up the ground of the summers before. The same move, made once a
   generation, is what [map-arc.md](map-arc.md) describes between lives;
   this is the smaller version of it, within one life.

Spending in the shop and giving to the family pull against each other, and
that is intended: an axe bought this winter is gold the family does not get,
and a level not reached means an item stays frosted a winter longer. What
the player buys never changes the level the shop uses, because the stock is
fixed at the level the winter began with. So a purchase can never close the
shop it was made in.

Upkeep grows only when a structure is finished, because the people working
it have to be supplied. Bridges and wells are not structures. A family level
never makes upkeep grow: it moves what upkeep is paid in, from fruit toward
gold, and the total falls.

## The list

Every line of upkeep, every item in the shop (frosted ones included) and the
family's next level has a box beside it, ticked by default. What a level
reached this winter opens has a box even in the first winter, when there is
no shop to show it: the box sits under the line announcing the level, so
reaching level 1 puts the axe on summer 2's list. The ticked lines become
the list the next summer shows across the top of the screen, one box per
line: what the player means to bring home. When there is only one way to go,
as in the first winter, the player never needs to touch a box. When there
are several, the boxes are how they choose.

The list is filled from the top: food in fruit, then rent, then each ticked
item, then the family's next level. Money is one pot, used up in that order.
An item's material is counted in kind: the axe's sticks as sticks. Gold is
counted from the money gathered, with feathers and shells at the price
winter will pay for them, and each gold amount is shown in feathers: a rent
of 10 gold is ten feathers. Building material does not sell, so carrying it
home never ticks off a gold box.

Every box looks the same, so the player picks the order. Each amount is a
row of squares, one per item: green at camp, amber when carried, and empty
when still to find, with a small tent at the start to show that green means
home. Gold is counted in feathers. The family's line is called "Increase
family wealth" and names only the gold: what its level opens is not in the
shop until the winter after, so the box does not promise it. In the first
summer only, the food line says in words what to do, "find 3, bring 4 to
camp". That is where the player learns that a thing counts once it is at
camp. A line whose amounts are all at camp turns dark green and stays that
way until the player closes it.

The first summer's list works the same way, with no shop: food, rent, and
the family's first level. It is the one piece of steering a new player gets,
and it names amounts, never places.

**Missing upkeep** does not end anything. The next summer is a tired one:
every action that takes time takes one and a half times as long, and rough
ground is walked at a quarter of walking speed instead of four tenths. Easy
ground is as fast as ever. That is felt on every wall and in every mud
pocket, it is gone by the following winter, and it is enough to make upkeep a
priority the year after.

## The start of a summer

Each summer starts with a small notice, and nothing more: how long this
summer is, and whether it is a tired one. What winter will need is on the
list. What grew back and what wore is found on the map.
